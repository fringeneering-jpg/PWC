# Light bending — push and pull (Jaden, 2026-10-02)

Reproduced by [`../light_bending_push_pull.py`](../light_bending_push_pull.py). Registry: `DERIVATIONS.md` row 14. The two terms are the two terms already listed in `OPEN_WORK.md` ("Light bending factor of 2"); what is new is why they are equal.

## Mechanism (his words in substance)
Two forces from different causes that happen to work together, both toward the mass:
1. **Pull** — the knot pulls (a photon of energy L has mass L/c₀², locked rule m = L/c₀²). A 1/r² pull on that mass gives a transverse impulse while the photon passes at impact parameter b: Δθ = (1/c₀²)∫g_perp dx = 2GM/(b·c₀²), independent of L.
2. **Push** — the medium around a mass is in equilibrium (its density fall is its own pull on itself): the pressure-gradient force per unit mass, (1/ρ)·dP/dr, equals the pull g. The photon is the same substance as the medium (its low-density phase) and follows the pressure gradient. Push = pull in size and direction.

Total inward acceleration 2g, total bend 4GM/(b·c₀²). No flowing medium, no index-of-refraction law, no spacetime metric.

## Numbers
Solar limb (GM/(Rc₀²) = 2.1225×10⁻⁶): pull 0.8756″ (numeric integral = analytic), push 0.8756″, total 1.7512″. Measured deflection ≈ 1.75″. In PPN terms the model gives γ = 1 exactly; the Cassini radio-link value γ − 1 = (2.1 ± 2.3)×10⁻⁵ (literature value, not re-checked this session) puts the model 0.9σ away. Every photon energy bends the same (L cancels), as observed.

## Honest limits
- The doubling rests on one assumption: the photon responds to the pressure gradient with the medium's own density (same substance). It is not independently measured.
- Only the factor p = 1 for the push is derived; nothing here tests a push of another size against data beyond the Cassini bound.
- Not shown: black-hole shadow sizes (Sgr A*, M87*). A flowing-medium (river-model) calculation gave GR's 3√3/2 r_s capture radius but is withdrawn — the medium does not flow — so the PWC shadow is undefined (OPEN).
- Pressure shadowing, polarization-dependent lensing (OPEN_WORK) are not addressed.
