# Independence audit of the 2026-09-30 closures

Generated by `PWC/audit_independence.py`. Each section states what the check
does and does not establish.

## 1. Where rho_0 comes from (T2)

rho_0 = 8.74e-27 kg/m^3 equals 0.95 * rho_crit at H0 = 69.985 km/s/Mpc.
  0.95 * rho_crit(70.0) = 8.7437e-27
So rho_0 is not independent of the Hubble constant: a measured H0 = 70 is
already inside it (paper/PWC_v1_draft.md: 'rho_0 ~ 0.95 rho_crit').
T2 is therefore an INPUT (DERIVATION_BRIEF: 'Option C accepted'), not a derivation.

## 2. H0_wall (T7) is not independent of the H0 inside rho_0

Gamma_untie = rho0*c0/(rho_max*R_p) = 2.392e-18 1/s  ->  73.81 km/s/Mpc
Because rho_0 = 0.95*rho_crit(H_in) and Gamma is linear in rho_0, H_out ~ H_in^2.

| H0 baked into rho_0 | H0_wall out | out/in |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 67.40 | 68.46 | 1.016 |
| 70.00 | 73.84 | 1.055 |
| 73.04 | 80.40 | 1.101 |

The output tracks whichever H0 was fed in: a Planck-valued input gives 68.5 (a 'match'
to Planck within 2%), a SH0ES-valued input gives 80.4. The input actually used, 70,
sits between them, so 73.8 landing within 1% of SH0ES does not discriminate anything.

## 4. The 'product invariant' and its stated precision

Unrounded framework product: 6.6100e-11 * 73.814 = 4.8791e-09
Rounded inputs as printed in the docs: 6.61e-11 * 73.8 = 4.8782e-09
Repo states 4.876e-9 (README, t7 doc) and 4.878e-9 (paper, proton doc), and the
match as 0.06% and 0.02%. The two 'precisions' differ by rounding of the same numbers.

| 'observed' pairing | product | framework / observed |
|---|---:|---:|
| a0=6.68e-11 (this repo's sqrt law), SH0ES 73.04 | 4.879e-09 | 1.000 |
| a0=6.68e-11, Planck 67.4 | 4.502e-09 | 1.084 |
| a0=1.2e-10 (McGaugh+2016 RAR form), SH0ES 73.04 | 8.765e-09 | 0.557 |
| a0=1.2e-10 (McGaugh+2016 RAR form), Planck 67.4 | 8.088e-09 | 0.603 |

The 0.06% figure holds for exactly one pairing. The observed H0 alone has a
~8% spread between SH0ES and Planck, and a0 is a fitted, functional-form-dependent
number, so 0.06% is not a measure of accuracy. Supportable claim: agreement at the
~10% level, with rho_0 (which embeds H0 = 70) and two chosen factors as inputs.

## 5. Proton identities

Identity A: m_p * R_p * c0 / hbar = 4.0008   (claimed 4)
Combined:   R_p = 0.8422 fm, m_p = 1.6707e-27 kg
rho_max implied by CODATA m_p and R_p through Identity B: 1.3093e+15 kg/m^3
  vs GW150914 calibration 1.304e+15  (ratio 1.0040)
Equivalent: (mean proton density / rho_max)^(1/3) = 8.0107   (claimed 8)

This is the strongest item in the batch: rho_max comes from GW150914 and the target is
proton data, so the inputs really are independent, and two dimensionless combinations
land within ~0.1% of the integers 4 and 8. Worth keeping and pursuing.
Limits: (i) the integers were attached by a topological story written after the numbers
were known, and the repo's own text says R_p spans 0.84-0.88 fm across methods (at
0.88 fm Identity A reads 4.184); (ii) which combinations to test was
also chosen after the fact (look-elsewhere); (iii) the same mechanism applied to the
neutron misses by ~2.5% (proton_derivation_20260930.md), over 20x worse than the proton
match, and is explained by 'isospin' only afterwards. Status: coincidence with a
proposed mechanism, not a derivation, until 4 and 8 are forced independently and a
second particle is predicted before its number is looked up.

## 6. Heat-spacer scan: can it fail?

Sign predicted from the input alone (-d2f/dd dT at d0, given f''>0) matches the
scan outcome in 540/540 rows, with no minimization needed to state it.
Implicit-function theorem: dd0/dT = -(d2f/dd dT) / f''(d0). Families A-D were written
with a negative mixed derivative and E with a positive one, so once f'' > 0 the sign
is fixed. The scan verifies the optimiser and the bookkeeping; it cannot fail on the
physics, and 432/432 + 108/108 is not evidence about PWC. A test that can fail needs a
thermal term whose d-dependence is derived from the medium, not chosen to point outward.

## 7. T2 fixed-point cell (values printed by the Colab run; the cell is not in the repo)

prefactor / required = 8.740000e-27  vs target 8.74e-27  (rel. diff 2.2e-16)
'required eta*(Delta_M/c0)' is the number that makes the fixed point equal the target,
so 'reproduced exactly' and 'residual 0.000' are identities, not confirmations.
Printed responses at +-1% of rho*: mean magnitude 2.38816e-20; B*0.01*rho* = 2.38816e-20.
The response and the linearised derivative are one calculation seen twice. Any
d(rho)/dt = source - sink(rho) with an increasing sink has a stable fixed point, so
'pushes toward the fixed point' holds for any B > 0 and cannot fail. The ODE itself is
not in the repo, so nothing beyond these relations can be audited here.
The real content is one number: eta*(Delta_M/c0) = 0.2488 must be <= 1 (v/c0 >= 0.2488).
That is a constraint on unknown microphysics, not a confirmation of it.
